The Privacy Commissioner publishes detailed rental guidance. This is a summary for tenants.

## At a viewing

Landlords can ask for your **name and contact details** only.

## When you apply

Landlords can ask for:

- proof of identity, and confirmation you're 18 or over
- the number of occupants, and the names of any occupants who won't be tenants
- contact details for landlord and non-landlord referees, plus your consent to contact them (they shouldn't contact them yet)
- your consent for a credit check and criminal record check (the checks themselves should only happen once you're negotiating an offer)
- whether you have pets or smoke, if the property has restrictions
- whether you have a legal right to stay in New Zealand, for fixed-term tenancies only

## At shortlisting or offer stage

Landlords can ask for:

- the details needed to run credit or criminal record checks, such as date of birth or a copy of ID
- **one** additional proof that you can pay the rent, such as a payslip, a letter from your employer or Work and Income, or proof of past rent payments. **You choose which.**

## What landlords should never ask

- About characteristics protected by the Human Rights Act, such as sex, relationship or family status, religion, ethnicity or nationality, disability, political opinion, employment status, sexual orientation or gender identity
- Whether you've experienced, or are experiencing, family violence
- About your spending, such as **bank statements showing transactions**
- Your employment history
- Your social media URLs

Landlords can look at public social media profiles, but should have a good reason, and can't ask to friend you or get a mutual friend to look.

Some of this can become relevant later in a tenancy. For example, if you ask for a rent reduction or a repayment plan, a landlord may reasonably ask about your other expenses.

## During the tenancy

Landlords can photograph the property at inspections to record its condition. Photos shouldn't be overly intrusive, especially in bedrooms, and shouldn't focus on your belongings.

## What you can do

- **You can refuse to give information a landlord shouldn't ask for**, and you shouldn't be disadvantaged for refusing.
- Landlords must tell you what they're collecting, why, who will see it, and what happens if you don't provide it.
- You can ask for a copy of the information a landlord or property manager holds about you, and ask for corrections. Our [request letter builder](/tools/request-letter/) helps.
- If you think you've been put on a tenant "blacklist", the Commissioner describes these as problematic under the Privacy Act. Raise it with the organisation first, then complain to the Privacy Commissioner.
