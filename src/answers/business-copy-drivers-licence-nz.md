## The Privacy Commissioner's view

The Commissioner has said for years that **simply looking at ID is usually enough**. In one case it accepted that a business needed to verify identity, but found that viewing the ID met that need, and that recording the licence number or photocopying the licence wasn't necessary. It has also considered a case about a bar that scanned and kept copies of patrons' driver licences.

The reasons are practical. Identity documents can't easily be changed if they leak, unlike a credit card. Every stored copy is a risk, as the large data breaches of recent years have shown.

## When a copy may be justified

- **Anti-money laundering checks.** Banks, lawyers, real estate agents, accountants and others covered by anti-money laundering law must verify identity and keep records for set periods.
- **Credit and tenancy checks.** A landlord may need ID details to run a credit or criminal record check once you're negotiating an offer.
- **Specific legal requirements** in other sectors.

Even then, the business should keep only what it needs, protect it, delete it when the retention period ends, and consider obscuring numbers it doesn't need.

## What a business should tell you

The Commissioner expects front-line staff to tell you why ID is needed, whether it will be copied, and how long a copy will be kept. If they can't answer, that's a sign they haven't thought about it.

## What you can do

- **Ask:** "Can you just look at it rather than copy it?" Many businesses will agree.
- **Ask why, and for how long**, if they insist on copying.
- **Offer an alternative**, such as showing ID that has fewer details, if they only need to confirm your age.
- **Ask them to delete it** after the check is done.
- **If your ID was exposed in a breach**, ask the business whether it will pay for a replacement, and consider a [credit file suppression](/guides/people/identity-theft/#credit).

## Facial recognition and ID scanners

If a venue or shop uses technology that analyses your face or matches you against a list, the Biometric Processing Privacy Code applies, with stricter rules. See our [biometrics guide](/guides/organisations/biometrics-code/).
