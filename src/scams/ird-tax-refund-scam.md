## How it works

Scammers impersonate Inland Revenue, and sometimes Police, by text, email or phone. Netsafe says the two common versions are:

- **"You're owed a refund"**: click a link and enter your bank details to receive it.
- **"You owe tax"**: pay now or face Police action.

Either way, the aim is your bank details, myIR login or identity information. Netsafe reported tax refund scam reports rose 44% in May 2026 compared with April, and warned they spike around tax time, from May to July.

## How to tell

Based on Netsafe's advice:

- **Real IRD emails** come from an address ending in **@ird.govt.nz**, and IRD won't include your refund amount in an email.
- **Real IRD texts** come from four-digit short codes. IRD doesn't text from ordinary mobile numbers.
- **IRD won't threaten arrest** on the phone, and won't ask you to log in to your bank during a call.
- **IRD never asks for payment by gift card**, iTunes card or prepaid card.
- **Don't click links** about refunds or payments. Log in to myIR directly through the IRD website.

If you're unsure about a call, hang up and call IRD yourself on **0800 775 247**.

## If you've been caught

1. **Call your bank** straight away if you shared bank or card details.
2. **Change your myIR password** if you entered it on a fake site, and anywhere you reused it.
3. **Get a new driver licence** through NZTA if you shared licence details, and consider a [credit file suppression](/guides/people/identity-theft/#credit).
4. **Contact IDCARE** (0800 121 068) for free identity support.
5. **Forward scam emails** to phishing@ird.govt.nz and scam texts to **7726**.
6. **Report to Netsafe**: 0508 638 723, text "Netsafe" to 4282, or online.
